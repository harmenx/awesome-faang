# Company Guides

How the largest technology companies run software engineering interviews, and what to emphasize for each.

> Formats change often and vary by team, level, and country. Use this page to plan, then confirm the exact rounds with your recruiter. Recruiters expect the question and will usually send a preparation guide.

## What every loop has in common

1. **Recruiter call.** Background, role, level, and logistics. Ask about the interview format, the level, and the timeline here.
2. **Screen.** An online assessment, one or two technical phone screens, or both.
3. **Loop.** Four to six interviews in a day or across a few days, covering coding, system design from mid-level up, and behavioral questions.
4. **Decision.** Interviewers submit written feedback, then a debrief or hiring committee decides the outcome and the level.
5. **Team match and offer.** At some companies you join a team after passing; at others you interview for one team from the start.

## Google

**Loop:** phone screen, then several coding interviews, system design for mid-level and above, and one "Googleyness and leadership" interview. Since 2025, Google includes at least one in-person round for engineering candidates.

**Distinctive:**

- A hiring committee, not your interviewers, makes the decision from written feedback. Clear communication matters because the committee only reads notes.
- Coding questions tend to be open-ended. Expect follow-ups that change the constraints once you have a working solution.
- Team matching usually happens after the committee approves you.

**Emphasize:** problem solving out loud, clean code, complexity analysis, and handling follow-ups. Graphs, trees, and dynamic programming come up often.

**Official:** [How Google Hires](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/how-we-hire)

## Meta

**Loop:** a coding screen, then coding interviews, a system or product design interview for mid-level and above, and a behavioral interview.

**Distinctive:**

- Coding rounds often include two problems in about 45 minutes, so speed and accuracy both matter.
- Since 2025, Meta has piloted an AI-assisted coding round for some candidates. Your recruiter will say in advance which rounds allow AI tools. Do not use them anywhere else.
- Many software engineering roles have historically joined a team after an onboarding period rather than at offer time.

**Emphasize:** fast, bug-free solutions to medium problems; arrays, strings, trees, and graphs; behavioral stories about impact, conflict, and growth.

**Official:** [Meta Careers](https://www.metacareers.com)

## Amazon

**Loop:** an online assessment, usually with coding problems and a work-style survey, then about four to five interviews. Each interviewer also asks about specific Leadership Principles.

**Distinctive:**

- The [Leadership Principles](behavioral.md#amazon-leadership-principles) are scored in every interview, so behavioral preparation matters as much as coding.
- One interviewer, the Bar Raiser, comes from outside the hiring team and can veto the hire.
- System design usually starts at SDE II. Senior loops add more design and leadership depth.

**Emphasize:** at least one strong story for every principle, with numbers; working, well-explained code over clever code; edge cases.

**Official:** [Leadership Principles](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/our-workplace/leadership-principles), [How Amazon Hires](https://www.amazon.jobs/content/en/how-we-hire)

## Apple

**Loop:** varies most by team. Expect a recruiter call, a manager or technical screen, then a loop that mixes coding, design, and domain questions for the team's area.

**Distinctive:**

- You interview for a specific team from the start, so questions often reflect its product area, such as operating systems, graphics, or services.
- Interviewers often probe deeply into projects on your resume.

**Emphasize:** fundamentals in the team's area, clear and careful code, and genuine interest in the product. Ask the recruiter what the team works on.

**Official:** [Apple Careers](https://www.apple.com/careers/us)

## Netflix

**Loop:** recruiter and hiring manager conversations, technical screens, then a loop of technical and culture interviews, usually for a specific team.

**Distinctive:**

- Hiring leans towards experienced engineers, and culture fit is weighted heavily.
- Interviewers test fit with the [culture memo](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture): freedom and responsibility, candor, and high performance.

**Emphasize:** system design and practical engineering depth for the team's domain; stories that show independent judgment and candid feedback.

**Official:** [Netflix Culture](https://jobs.netflix.com/culture)

## Microsoft

**Loop:** a screen, then about four to five interviews, mostly coding with design questions for experienced roles. Formats vary by organization.

**Distinctive:**

- You often interview with the team you would join, so questions can reflect its product.
- A senior interviewer frequently closes the loop and weighs the overall decision.

**Emphasize:** clean, working code at medium difficulty, clear communication, and collaboration.

**Official:** [Microsoft Careers](https://careers.microsoft.com)

## Preparing for several companies at once

- The core is shared. Coding patterns, system design fundamentals, and a strong story bank prepare you for all of them.
- Add a company-specific layer in the last week or two before each loop: Amazon's principles, Meta's pace, Google's follow-ups.
- Schedule your most-wanted company last, after a practice loop or two elsewhere.
- Line up onsites and offers close together. Competing offers are the strongest lever in [negotiation](../README.md#offers-and-negotiation).
